A research analysis.
A clear scientific pitch.

Our presentation analyses an existing article. We start with the human and economic impact of Alzheimer’s, explain the study’s findings, and examine their importance for treatment research and other diseases.

What are they asking for?

The Life Sciences challenge asks how to distinguish cells that are adapting or compensating from cells that are losing function. It accepts an analysis, model, experiment design or prototype, supported by evidence.

Our article identifies spatial associations with inhibitory neuron gene profiles. It does not establish what those cells are doing. This gives us a relevant starting point for the challenge: a reason to investigate these cells, with a clear account of what the scans do and do not tell us.

Read the official Life Sciences brief

How should we present it?

  1. Start with the need.

    Explain how many people are affected, how the number is expected to grow and what the disease costs. Distinguish the medicines market from the wider burden of care.

    Show: the US and global figures, their years and their sources.

  2. Explain the discovery.

    Start with the human question: why are some parts of the brain more affected than others? Show how we compared brain tissue loss with receptor maps and cellular gene profiles. Explain the 5-HT1A and SST findings in plain language.

    Show: the study population, the analysis and the main findings.

  3. Explain the treatment opportunity.

    Describe how a biological association can give researchers a direction for drug studies. Be clear that the article did not test a medicine or prove that changing a receptor would help patients.

    Show: why the finding matters and what evidence would be needed before a treatment claim.

  4. Explain the broader method.

    The earlier published work also studied frontotemporal dementia and mild cognitive impairment. The method can be investigated in other conditions using suitable data, with separate validation for each disease.

    Show: the diseases already studied and the applications that remain future possibilities.

What still needs to be decided for the submission?

A clear pitch can explain the article, but the event also asks for an inspectable contribution and supporting evidence. We need to state exactly what we contribute during the hackathon, how another team can check it, and which work already existed.

The agreed focus is the article analysis and pitch. We should describe the interpretation and presentation work honestly, without presenting the earlier study as research completed during this event.
